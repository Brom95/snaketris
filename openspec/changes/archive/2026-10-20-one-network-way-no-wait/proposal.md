## Why

The network section carries two ways and a wait that only one of them uses. The anywhere-network way depends on a public candidate supply this project never provides — no relay is built, and measured attempts with no servers pair nobody outside one browser instance — while the same-network way names an address and a port itself. The candidate wait belongs to that supply: a page that opened a link never finished it (measured, the countdown stuck at three for the whole run, and one page's own port sat unread behind it). Keeping both ways keeps a wall of lines — the wait, its countdown, its warnings, the address a way cannot type — that the way a player can actually join does not use.

## What Changes

- The network section offers ONE way: the same-network pair. The anywhere-network item is gone from the way menu, its copy, its markup and its branching.
- No page runs a candidate attempt at all: the wait, its countdown line, its warning lines and its timeout constant are retired, along with the attempt's functions and the public candidate supply it read.
- A page reads its own port out of its own description, publishes its line, and aims at the peer its line named — every page, including the one that opened a link, before any other pairing work.
- The pairing step keeps what the one way uses: its link and QR, its address field and copy affordance, its carried line and field, its waiting line.
- The public candidate supply is no longer read from the page's own address; no ICE server is configured at all.
